## What the peer plans have in common

Structure (identical across all three): Overview (aspiration, goals, excitement, strengths, energizing contributions) →
"How am I developing the relevant skills" by dimension → mentors/sponsors → Looking Ahead (2 dimensions) → support needed →
energy/sustainability → optional DGL topics.

Dimensions used: Manages Work/Projects, Solves Problems, Counsels Clients / Builds Relationships, Communicates Effectively,
Builds Client Capabilities, Demonstrates Entrepreneurship, Builds Expertise, Enriches Firm Talent, Apprentices Others,
Upholds Values, Demonstrates Resilience. Use the ones you have real evidence for; drop the rest.

Bullet formula (strongest in the Associate-level plan): **verb + what you did + scale/number + "; this" + impact for the client or Firm.**
- Weak: "Supported the client with the business case"
- Strong: "Built a monetization case with 20 Mn AZN annual value and presented it directly to the Minister; this clarified the economic upside of the program"

Other patterns worth copying:
- Name the seniority of who you worked with (Minister, CEO, CHRO) and how often (weekly, day to day).
- Quantify team size, number of sessions, use cases, interviews, people reached.
- Signal operating one level up (e.g. "owned the workstream without an EM", "JEM'ing").
- "Looking Ahead" is candid about the next growth area and links it to the support you ask for (e.g. a larger team, a new industry).
- The mid-cycle plan groups bullets by project when you were on several; the annual review leads with the biggest outcomes.

5. Firm building: recruiting interviews, PDs/knowledge, office initiatives, coaching juniors.
6. Development feedback from your last review and what you did about it.
7. Your aspiration (next milestone, MBA, expertise area) and what support you need.
8. Mentors/sponsors (as "First name + last initial").
